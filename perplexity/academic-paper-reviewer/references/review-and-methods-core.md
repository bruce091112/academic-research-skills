# Review and Methodology Core — Perplexity Edition

This reference preserves the reusable review-target and methodology-audit logic of the original Academic Research Skills while removing repository-specific runtime bindings.

## 1. Separate three questions

Every review must distinguish:

1. **Scientific/methodological validity** — Is the study design and reasoning sound?
2. **Target fit** — Does the manuscript fit the explicitly confirmed journal, venue, track, assignment, or evaluation context?
3. **Submission readiness** — Is the work complete enough to submit or hand off?

Do not convert target-fit advice into a scientific-validity finding, and do not infer a target the user has not confirmed.

## 2. Review target authority

Use only author-confirmed target information. Do not infer journal, track, article type, jurisdiction, or review standard from language, filename, institution, or topic.

If target criteria conflict, keep them parallel rather than averaging or silently choosing the easier criterion.

## 3. Reviewer roles

When useful, divide review into distinct roles:
- editorial/overall fit;
- methodology;
- domain/substantive expertise;
- alternative perspective or interdisciplinary lens;
- adversarial/devil's-advocate review.

All roles should evaluate the same artifact version and the same confirmed review target.

## 4. Constructive findings

For every Critical or Major finding, provide:
- exact issue;
- manuscript evidence or absence anchor;
- why it matters;
- minimum viable remedy;
- stronger but more costly remedy when meaningful;
- likely trade-off or effort;
- whether the remedy would require new data, new analysis, or a change in author intent;
- whether no honest remedy exists without additional evidence.

Never imply that missing data, analyses, approvals, or results already exist.

## 5. Methodology alignment audit

Audit the following chain:

`RQ / hypothesis → theory / construct → operationalization → instrument or data source → analysis → finding → conclusion`

For each RQ, verify:
- at least one data source can answer it;
- the relevant construct is operationalized;
- the analysis actually addresses the question;
- findings are traceable to the stated data and analysis;
- conclusions do not exceed the design's inferential scope.

Also flag:
- data collected but never used;
- findings with no corresponding RQ or declared exploratory aim;
- constructs introduced only in the conclusion;
- instruments that measure a related but different construct;
- triangulation claims based on non-independent evidence streams;
- reflexive/teacher/researcher notes being treated as participant evidence without distinction.

## 6. Survey and psychometric safeguards

When designing or auditing rating-scale items:

### True reverse-coded item
A true reverse-coded item must measure the **same construct** on the **same response dimension** as the positively keyed items. If reverse scoring would not meaningfully align the item with the focal scale, it is a contrast item, not a reverse-coded item.

For every item labeled reverse-coded, require a one-line construct-equivalence justification.

### Acquiescence
Do not claim acquiescence-bias mitigation merely because an instrument contains negatively worded items. The negatively keyed items must pass the construct-equivalence test.

### Recall bias
Prefer event-anchored retrospective items when possible. Calendar windows without a shared salient anchor are more vulnerable to recall drift and telescoping.

## 7. Human-subjects and authority boundary

Human-subjects, IRB/REC, privacy, and jurisdictional questions require explicit context. Do not infer legal or ethical applicability from locale, affiliation, filenames, or model memory.

Treat official rules and laws as bounded authority sources, not complete legal advice. If jurisdiction, institutional overlay, or applicability is unknown, say so.

Never claim:
- ethics approval was obtained;
- exemption applies;
- legal compliance is established;
- a submission packet is institutionally acceptable;

unless the user supplies authoritative evidence supporting that statement.

## 8. Severity vocabulary

Use severity only for scholarly consequences:
- **Critical** — threatens validity, integrity, or interpretability of the central contribution;
- **Major** — materially weakens a key claim, method, or inference;
- **Minor** — local issue that does not alter the main conclusion;
- **Advisory** — optional improvement, target-fit note, or unresolved concern.

Severity is not the same as venue preference.

## 9. Author control

Reviewer recommendations do not authorize changes to research intent. A reviewer may identify a problem and propose remedies, but changes to RQs, constructs, analytic strategy, interpretation, or scope remain author decisions.
