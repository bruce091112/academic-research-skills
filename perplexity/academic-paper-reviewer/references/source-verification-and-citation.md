# Source Verification & Citation Core — Perplexity Edition

This reference preserves the original source-verification logic while removing dependence on specific APIs or local validators.

## 1. Trust but verify

No source is automatically trusted because it appears scholarly, highly cited, institutional, or familiar. Verification should be proportional to how consequential the claim is.

For each important source, inspect as available:
- publication venue and publisher legitimacy;
- author identity / affiliation;
- bibliographic consistency;
- DOI or stable identifier;
- publication status;
- retraction / correction / supersession status;
- method quality and sample fit;
- disclosed funding and conflicts of interest;
- date relevance for the field.

## 2. Reference existence verification

Prefer independent bibliographic confirmation through at least one stable source such as:
- DOI resolution;
- Crossref-like bibliographic metadata;
- Semantic Scholar-like scholarly index;
- publisher page;
- library / database record;
- exact-title web search when structured metadata is unavailable.

When identifiers disagree with title, author, year, or venue, flag the mismatch for human review.

Do not fabricate bibliographic fields to make a reference look complete.

## 3. Verification status

Use one of these source-existence states:
- **VERIFIED** — stable identifier or authoritative bibliographic record matches key metadata;
- **PLAUSIBLE** — existence confirmed through reputable secondary bibliographic evidence but not fully resolved;
- **UNVERIFIABLE** — insufficient accessible evidence to confirm;
- **MISMATCH** — identifier or key metadata resolves to a different work;
- **FABRICATED / NON-EXISTENT** — strong evidence that the cited work does not exist as claimed.

`UNVERIFIABLE` is not the same as `FABRICATED`.

## 4. Claim verification is separate from source existence

A real paper may fail to support the claim attributed to it.

For every consequential claim, distinguish:
- source exists;
- source says something relevant;
- source directly supports the claim as written;
- source supports only part of the claim;
- source conflicts with the claim;
- claim remains unsupported.

Use the shared E1–E5 evidence states from `research-integrity-core.md`.

## 5. Evidence hierarchy is context-sensitive

Do not apply one universal hierarchy mechanically across disciplines.

For intervention-effect questions, systematic reviews and randomized studies may carry more causal weight. For qualitative meaning, lived experience, cultural interpretation, or ethnographic context, a well-conducted qualitative study may be the appropriate primary evidence rather than a low-grade substitute.

Grade source quality by fit to the specific claim and research question, not by design label alone.

## 6. Currency

Assess source age relative to field velocity:
- fast-moving technical / policy topics may require very recent evidence;
- education and organizational research may tolerate a wider window;
- historical and foundational theory may remain relevant indefinitely.

Never exclude a seminal source solely for age. Never treat a recent source as superior solely because it is recent.

## 7. Venue and predatory-journal caution

Potential warning signs include:
- unclear editorial board;
- misleading metrics;
- implausibly rapid acceptance;
- journal scope inconsistent with the article;
- unverifiable ISSN / publisher information;
- aggressive solicitation;
- copied or incoherent website content.

These are warning signals, not automatic proof. Report the basis for the concern.

Do not use Beall-style lists as sole evidence of predatory status.

## 8. Conflict-of-interest transparency

Consider financial, institutional, intellectual, personal, and political relationships when they are relevant to interpretation.

A conflict-of-interest flag does not automatically invalidate evidence. It changes the interpretation context and may increase the need for triangulation.

## 9. Cross-source verification

For important factual claims, triangulate across independent sources where feasible.

Independence matters. Five papers from the same dataset, lab, review family, or press release chain are not equivalent to five independent evidence streams.

When sources conflict:
- preserve the disagreement;
- compare methods, samples, time periods, and definitions;
- avoid majority-vote logic based only on source count;
- state which evidence is stronger and why.

## 10. Citation discipline

Every in-text citation should be traceable to a real reference entry. Every reference entry should be cited or explicitly retained for a documented reason.

Do not:
- cite a source for a claim it does not support;
- move a citation across sentences if that changes what it appears to support;
- attach a background citation to current-study results;
- convert an abstract-only source into a full-text claim without labeling the limitation;
- invent page numbers, quotation marks, or quotation text.

## 11. Direct quotations

Quote only text that has been directly verified from the source. Preserve wording, scope, negation, and relevant qualifiers. If exact text cannot be verified, paraphrase and label the access limitation.

## 12. Output expectations

A verification report should distinguish at least:
- source-existence status;
- bibliographic issues;
- claim-support status;
- methodological limitations;
- currency;
- COI / funding context;
- unresolved questions;
- recommended use: primary support, supporting context, caveated use, or exclude from evidentiary role.

Red flags should be visible rather than silently filtering sources out.
