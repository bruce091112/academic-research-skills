# Systematic Review & Risk of Bias Core — Perplexity Edition

This reference preserves systematic-review and study-level bias-assessment methodology while removing assumptions about local scripts or specialized runtime tooling.

## 1. Systematic-review protocol before synthesis

Before screening, define:
- review question and PICOS/PICo/SPIDER or other suitable framework;
- databases/search surfaces;
- eligibility criteria;
- screening procedure;
- data-extraction fields;
- risk-of-bias tool by study design;
- synthesis plan;
- subgroup/sensitivity analyses when applicable;
- preregistration/protocol status.

Do not present a post-hoc search and selection process as preregistered or prospectively specified.

## 2. Study-design classification precedes risk-of-bias tool selection

Use the validated tool appropriate to design.

### Randomized trials
Use RoB 2, including the relevant extension for cluster or crossover trials when applicable.

Core domains:
1. randomization process;
2. deviations from intended interventions;
3. missing outcome data;
4. outcome measurement;
5. selection of the reported result.

Judgments: Low Risk / Some Concerns / High Risk.

### Non-randomized intervention studies
Use ROBINS-I when applicable.

Core domains:
1. confounding;
2. participant selection;
3. intervention classification;
4. deviations from intended interventions;
5. missing data;
6. outcome measurement;
7. selection of the reported result.

Judgments: Low / Moderate / Serious / Critical Risk / No Information.

Do not invent a hybrid score that collapses validated domains into an unsupported numeric quality score.

## 3. Signaling questions first

For every domain:
- answer the tool's signaling questions before assigning the domain judgment;
- cite the exact study passage, table, appendix, registry, protocol, or explicit absence used;
- distinguish `No Information` from evidence that the practice did not occur;
- preserve outcome-specific judgments when the instrument requires them.

Insufficient reporting is not equivalent to proof of high bias, but it limits confidence and must remain visible.

## 4. Overall judgment follows the instrument

Do not override the validated aggregation logic based on global impressions.

RoB 2: a High Risk domain normally drives overall High Risk; Some Concerns without High Risk yields overall Some Concerns.

ROBINS-I: overall judgment reflects the most serious domain judgment under the official framework.

When unsure about exact current instrument wording or algorithms, consult the authoritative current documentation rather than relying on memory.

## 5. Risk of bias is not the same as study relevance

A highly relevant study may have substantial bias. A low-bias study may address the review question only indirectly. Keep separate:
- relevance/applicability;
- methodological risk of bias;
- reporting completeness;
- precision;
- source existence/integrity.

## 6. Mixed-methods studies

Assess components using appropriate frameworks rather than forcing one tool across the whole study.

For qualitative components, use an appropriate qualitative appraisal approach and preserve the distinction between methodological appraisal and quantitative RoB judgments.

## 7. Bias assessment must affect synthesis transparently

Use study-level bias judgments to inform:
- sensitivity analyses;
- certainty assessment;
- interpretation of conflicting findings;
- whether a pooled estimate is robust to excluding high-risk studies.

Do not silently delete high-risk studies merely to improve the pooled result. Report inclusion/exclusion decisions and sensitivity results.

## 8. PRISMA and reporting

For a systematic review, retain a transparent record of identification, screening, eligibility, inclusion, exclusions with reasons, and synthesis decisions.

Use the current applicable PRISMA guidance and extensions when claiming PRISMA alignment. Do not call a review systematic merely because it contains many sources.

## 9. Output

A risk-of-bias report should include:
- study and design;
- instrument used;
- domain judgments;
- signaling-question evidence;
- overall judgment;
- unresolved information;
- implications for synthesis.

A review-level summary may use traffic-light presentation, but symbols are presentation only; the domain reasoning remains authoritative.
