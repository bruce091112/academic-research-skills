# Literature Search & Screening Core — Perplexity Edition

This reference preserves the literature-search methodology of the original Academic Research Skills while replacing API- and runtime-specific mechanics with platform-neutral research rules.

## 1. Search must be systematic, not ad hoc

For each literature task, record:

- research question or review objective;
- target databases / search surfaces;
- date searched;
- keywords, synonyms, controlled vocabulary where applicable;
- Boolean strategy;
- date range and justification;
- language limits;
- document types;
- inclusion criteria;
- exclusion criteria.

Criteria should be defined before screening whenever possible. Do not retroactively tune criteria merely to keep favored studies.

## 2. Breadth before depth

Start broad enough to identify the vocabulary, seminal work, competing traditions, and relevant methods. Narrow only after the first retrieval pass reveals the literature structure.

Search expansion may include:
- synonym expansion;
- alternate spellings and translations;
- adjacent theoretical constructs;
- backward citation chasing;
- forward citation chasing;
- author / research-group follow-up;
- venue or review-article snowballing.

Do not confuse search breadth with inclusion breadth. A broad search can still have strict inclusion criteria.

## 3. Two-pass screening

### Pass 1 — title/abstract screening

Classify each record as:
- include for full-text review;
- exclude, with reason;
- uncertain / needs full text.

### Pass 2 — full-text assessment

Apply the same criteria more rigorously and document reasons for exclusion.

Never report an abstract-only judgment as a full-text finding unless explicitly labeled as abstract-level evidence.

## 4. Inclusion/exclusion transparency

At minimum, assess:
- direct relevance to the RQ;
- population / context fit;
- method or study-design fit;
- outcome / construct fit;
- date-range fit;
- language fit;
- publication / document type;
- availability of enough source content to support the intended use.

Availability is not equivalent to quality. Lack of full text may limit verification without proving that the study is poor.

## 5. Deduplication

Deduplicate using the strongest identifiers available:
1. DOI or stable paper identifier;
2. exact / near-exact title + author + year;
3. preprint vs. final publication family;
4. conference vs. journal extension, when clearly the same work family.

When two records are variants of one work, prefer the version with the most complete and authoritative bibliographic metadata, but preserve version differences when they materially change content.

## 6. Corpus-first, search-fills-gap

When the user supplies a literature corpus:

1. screen the user corpus against the current RQ using the same criteria as external search;
2. do not silently drop user-supplied sources;
3. record excluded or unusable entries with a reason;
4. identify uncovered RQ subtopics;
5. search externally to fill those gaps unless the user explicitly requests corpus-only analysis;
6. merge the screened corpus and external results without giving either automatic priority.

Presence in the user corpus does not imply inclusion. External search does not override user-provided primary sources merely because it is newer.

## 7. Coverage and distribution audit

After screening, inspect whether the included literature is narrowly concentrated in ways that may bias interpretation.

Check only dimensions supported by known metadata:
- time period;
- geography / study context;
- methodology;
- population;
- venue family / grey literature / preprint concentration;
- theoretical tradition, when explicitly identifiable.

Treat concentration as an advisory coverage signal, not an automatic defect.

Useful rule of thumb: when one known category accounts for roughly 70% or more of known entries, surface the concentration and ask whether the RQ justifies it. Use the denominator of known values for that dimension, not total N.

## 8. PRISMA-style accounting when appropriate

For systematic or structured reviews, record:

- records identified by source;
- duplicates removed;
- records screened;
- records excluded at title/abstract stage;
- full texts assessed;
- full texts excluded, with reasons;
- final studies included.

Do not claim PRISMA compliance unless the actual workflow and reporting meet the applicable guideline.

## 9. Annotated bibliography fields

For each included source, capture at least:
- full citation;
- relevance to RQ;
- key findings;
- method / sample / context;
- limitations;
- evidence quality or confidence;
- contribution to the current synthesis;
- verification status.

Do not turn the annotated bibliography into the synthesis itself. Source-by-source notes remain Phase-2 evidence organization; cross-source interpretation belongs in synthesis.

## 10. Search reproducibility and limitations

Every search report should state:
- what was searched;
- when it was searched;
- what could not be accessed;
- what was omitted by design;
- known database / language / date / indexing limitations;
- whether the search was systematic, structured, rapid, or exploratory.

Never call a search exhaustive when the search surface cannot justify that claim.
