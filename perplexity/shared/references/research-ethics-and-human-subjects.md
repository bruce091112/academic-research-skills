# Research Ethics & Human-Subjects Boundary — Perplexity Edition

This reference preserves the research-ethics safeguards of the original Academic Research Skills while keeping institutional/legal authority with the responsible human bodies.

## 1. AI-assisted research disclosure

When AI materially contributes to research or writing, document as appropriate:
- tool/model used;
- task scope (search, screening, synthesis, drafting, editing, translation, analysis, etc.);
- human verification/oversight;
- limitations relevant to reproducibility or reliability;
- date/version when materially relevant.

Follow the target journal/institution's current policy rather than assuming one universal disclosure format.

## 2. Attribution integrity

Require:
- no fabricated references;
- direct quotes reproduced exactly and attributed;
- paraphrases that preserve meaning rather than merely rearranging words;
- attribution to the original source when available;
- in-text citations and reference list reconciled;
- no source listed as support for a claim it does not support.

## 3. Conflict of interest

Consider financial, institutional, intellectual, personal, political, commercial, and tool-provider relationships when they could influence design, analysis, interpretation, or presentation.

Disclosure is distinct from proof of bias. Flag the relationship and its possible relevance rather than presuming misconduct.

## 4. Fair representation

For contested, culturally specific, or community-based research:
- represent relevant perspectives accurately;
- avoid deficit or stigmatizing framing;
- make researcher power and positionality visible when material;
- do not generalize one community's governance framework to another;
- preserve distinctions between scholarly evidence, participant voice, community knowledge, and researcher interpretation.

## 5. Data ethics

Document, when applicable:
- source provenance;
- access/use basis or unresolved permission questions;
- personal-data handling;
- identifiers and quasi-identifiers;
- platform metadata such as IP, device identifiers, timestamps, contact fields, or cookies;
- access roles and recipients;
- retention/deletion plans;
- reuse/sharing conditions;
- security and incident-handling considerations.

Do not claim anonymity, de-identification, legal basis, or authorization unless the evidence supports that exact status.

## 6. Human-subjects boundary

The skill may help organize facts, risks, materials, and questions. It must not act as an IRB/REC/ethics committee, institutional officer, data-protection authority, or legal decision maker.

Do not infer review pathway, exemption, approval, waiver, consent sufficiency, legal basis, or institutional authorization from:
- the research topic;
- geography alone;
- language;
- affiliation;
- manuscript wording;
- model memory.

Use explicit user/institutional facts and current authoritative requirements.

When jurisdiction, authority, or institutional process is unresolved, state:
- institutional determination required;
- authorization status not verified;
- unresolved applicability or documentation gaps.

## 7. Participant-facing materials

When reviewing consent/information materials, distinguish:
- clarity/readability;
- apparent coverage of stated study information;
- actual institutional/legal adequacy.

The first two may be assessed as drafting quality. The third requires the responsible authority.

Do not treat an electronic `I agree` interaction as automatically equivalent to legally/institutionally sufficient consent or signature.

## 8. Dual-use and harm review

Assess concrete enabling detail, not mere subject matter.

A sensitive topic is not by itself a dual-use problem. Escalate concern when the work provides operational details that materially lower barriers to harmful action.

Research ethics review should not suppress legitimate critical, accountability, historical, or public-interest research merely because it studies harmful systems.

## 9. Reproducibility ethics

Document enough to allow scholarly scrutiny:
- search strategy;
- eligibility criteria;
- analytic method;
- data transformations;
- AI-assisted steps where material;
- code/scripts or equivalent procedure when available;
- deviations from planned methods.

Do not claim reproducibility when required data, code, methods, or access are unavailable.

## 10. Researcher positionality and dual roles

When the researcher is also teacher, practitioner, manager, designer, clinician, community member, or evaluator, record how that role may affect:
- recruitment and voluntariness;
- participant responses;
- observation;
- interpretation;
- intervention delivery;
- coding and theme formation.

Treat reflexivity as part of analytic transparency, not as a ceremonial appendix.

## 11. Fixed boundary language for uncertain human-subjects cases

When needed, use a concise boundary such as:

> This analysis can identify methodological and documentation issues, but it does not authorize recruitment, consent, access to identifiable data, intervention, or data collection. Institutional determination is required where applicable.
